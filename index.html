Project Overview & Research Scope

An experimental evaluation of graph-theoretic single-linkage clustering versus centroidal partitioning under controlled stress testing.

Official Research Objective

Compare Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) based clustering and K-Means clustering under noise, outliers, and non-linear data distributions using quantitative robustness measures.

While standard centroidal algorithms like K-Means are widely adopted due to linear computational scaling, their fundamental assumption of isotropic, convex clusters causes acute failure on arbitrary geometric manifolds. Conversely, MST-based clustering naturally traverses non-linear geometries via graph connectivity, but exhibits extreme sensitivity to single-linkage noise bridges.

K-Means Clustering: Lloyd's Heuristic with K-Means++

  1. Seeded K-Means++ Initialization: Selects the first centroid c1 uniformly at random from dataset. Successive centroids cj are sampled with probability proportional to squared distance D(x)² to the nearest chosen centroid, preventing poor local minima.
  2. Voronoi Assignment: Every point xi is assigned to its closest centroid in Euclidean space: yi = argmink ||xi - ck||².
  3. Centroid Mean Update: Each centroid is recomputed as the arithmetic mean of its assigned points: ck = (1 / |Sk|) ∑ xi.
  4. Empty Cluster Handling: If a cluster loses all members (|Sk| = 0), it is re-seeded at the data point with maximal squared distance to its assigned center.
  5. Convergence Criterion: Iterates until centroid movement ||Δc|| < 10-5 or maximum iteration count (100) is reached.

Euclidean Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) Clustering

  1. Complete Graph Formulation: Treats all N points as vertices in a complete geometric graph G = (V, E) where edge weights equal Euclidean distances d(pi, pj).
  2. Prim's Dense Construction: Greedily grows a tree from vertex 0 in O(N²) time, continuously attaching the non-tree vertex with minimum distance to the current tree until exactly N - 1 edges are selected.
  3. K - 1 Longest Edge Cut: Sorts the N - 1 tree edges in descending order of Euclidean length and deletes the K - 1 longest edges.
  4. Connected Component Extraction: Uses Disjoint Set Union (DSU / Union-Find) with path compression and rank optimization to identify the remaining K connected components.
  5. Topological Invariance: Because MST edges follow shortest local point-to-point connections, it traces non-linear manifolds regardless of curvature.

Comparative Inductive Biases & Known Failure Modes

K-Means Failure: Non-Convex Geometries

K-Means partitions space into linear convex polyhedra (Voronoi cells). When clusters are shaped as concentric rings or interlocking crescents, no linear boundary can separate them. K-Means forcibly cuts through the crescents to minimize within-cluster variance.

MST Failure: Chaining & Outlier Isolation

Because MST clustering relies on single linkage, a few random noise points positioned between two distinct manifolds form a "stepping stone" bridge. Furthermore, extreme isolated outliers generate long pendant edges; cutting the K-1 longest edges often lops off single outliers as singleton clusters, leaving the main clusters merged!
