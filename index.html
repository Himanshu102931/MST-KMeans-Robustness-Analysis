Project Overview & Research Scope

An experimental evaluation of graph-theoretic single-linkage clustering versus centroidal partitioning under controlled stress testing.

Official Research Objective

Compare Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) based clustering and K-Means clustering under noise, outliers, and non-linear data distributions using quantitative robustness measures.

While standard centroidal algorithms like K-Means are widely adopted due to linear computational scaling, their fundamental assumption of isotropic, convex clusters causes acute failure on arbitrary geometric manifolds. Conversely, MST-based clustering naturally traverses non-linear geometries via graph connectivity, but exhibits extreme sensitivity to single-linkage noise bridges.

Experimental Methodology & Mathematical Rigor

1. Synthetic Data Geometries

2. Outlier Tracking & Inlier ARI

Ground-truth reference labels are strictly reserved for post-clustering evaluation. Injected uniform outliers are tagged to compute true manifold recovery via Inlier Adjusted Rand Index (Inlier ARI).

3. Deterministic Stress Testing

Controlled variance in Gaussian noise (σ) and outlier density fraction to observe the exact phase transition where single-linkage chaining and centroid pulling occur.

Theoretical Computational Complexity Comparison

Algorithm Time Complexity Space Complexity Scalability Bottleneck Inductive Bias
K-Means (Lloyd's) O(N · K · I) O(N + K) Linear in N; efficient for large sample sizes. Spherical, equal-variance convex clusters.
Euclidean MST (Prim's Dense) O(N²) O(N) Quadratic pairwise distance comparisons. Spatial connectivity; single-linkage manifolds.

Comparative Inductive Biases & Vulnerabilities

K-Means: Voronoi Convex Partitioning

Minimizes within-cluster sum of squares: argmin ∑ ||x - μk||². This inductive bias restricts boundaries to linear Voronoi hyperplanes, which inevitably cuts through non-convex manifolds (e.g. concentric circles and interlocking crescent moons). However, centroid averaging provides natural robustness against sparse random bridging noise.

MST Clustering: Single-Linkage Chaining & Outlier Breakdown

Constructs a Minimum Spanning Tree in O(N²) via Prim's algorithm, then cuts the K - 1 longest edges. While this effortlessly traces arbitrary non-linear manifolds, it exhibits extreme vulnerability to single-linkage chaining: a single noise point between two clusters creates a short-distance bridge that merges them. Furthermore, isolated outliers produce long pendant edges; removing the longest edges isolates outliers into singleton clusters while leaving true clusters unseparated!
