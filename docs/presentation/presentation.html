Motivation & Context

The Challenge of Unsupervised Clustering

Uncovering latent grouping without supervision

• The Algorithmic Dilemma

  • Inductive Bias: Clustering algorithms impose strict mathematical assumptions on cluster shapes.
  • Centroid Models (K-Means): Assume spatial convexity, spherical compactness, and linear hyperplane separability.
  • Graph Models (MST): Assume continuous topological proximity traced via single-linkage paths.
  • The Conflict: When data deviates from spherical ideals, centroid methods fail structurally.

• Real-World Robustness

  • Physical Sensors: Produce isotropic thermal and Gaussian measurement noise ($\sigma$).
  • Anomaly Contamination: Background noise and uncalibrated readings create ambient outliers ($\eta$).
  • Non-Linear Topologies: Biological cell trajectories, GIS corridors, and spiral structures are non-convex.
  • Our Research Goal: Quantitatively determine exact breakdown boundaries under controlled perturbations.

Centroid Paradigm

Lloyd's K-Means & K-Means++ Seeding

Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS) minimization

• Mathematical Formulation

Minimizes Inertia (Within-Cluster Sum of Squares):

J = ∑k=1K ∑xi ∈ Ck || xi - μk ||2
  • K-Means++ Seeding: Sample $\mu_k$ with probability $P(x) \propto D(x)^2$; guarantees $O(\log K)$ approximation bound.
  • Assignment: $l_i = \arg\min_k \|x_i - \mu_k\|^2$ (Voronoi partitioning).
  • Update: $\mu_k = \frac{1}{|C_k|} \sum x_i$ (Gradient stationary point).
  • Convergence: Stop when centroid shift $< 10^{-5}$ or $I \ge 100$.

• Mechanics & Geometric Bias

  • Noise Averaging Advantage: Centroid calculation is an arithmetic mean. Sample mean variance is $\sigma^2 / |C_k|$, suppressing zero-mean Gaussian noise by $1/N_k$.
  • The Linear Hyperplane Constraint: Decision boundaries between any two clusters are hyperplanes $\{x : \|x - \mu_A\| = \|x - \mu_B\|\}$.
  • Consequence: Incapable of bending around curved crescent arcs or separating nested concentric spheres.
Graph Paradigm

Prim's MST & Graph-Theoretic Cuts

Single-linkage spanning forest partitioning

• Minimum Spanning Tree Construction

Complete Euclidean Graph $G = (V, E)$, $|V| = N$:

w(T) = ∑e ∈ T || u - v ||2 → Minimized
  • Prim's Dense Algorithm: Greedily adds minimum-weight cut edge using distance array $\kappa[v]$. Time complexity: $\Theta(N^2)$.
  • Zahn's Partitioning: Sort all $N-1$ tree edges in descending order; delete $(K-1)$ longest edges.
  • Subtree Extraction: Disjoint Set Union (Union-Find) with path compression labels the $K$ connected components.

• Topological Transitivity & Fragility

  • Manifold Discovery: Connects points through chains of short pairwise Euclidean steps, regardless of non-linear shape.
  • Single-Linkage Vulnerability: Distance between clusters is defined by nearest neighbor pair: $d(A, B) = \min_{u \in A, v \in B} \|u - v\|$.
  • The Outlier Trap: A single intermediate noise point bridges disparate clusters, while isolated outliers hijack tree cuts.

Rigorous Evaluation

Controlled Methodology & Inlier Metric

Strict separation of noise and outlier variables

• Controlled Perturbation Protocol

  • Experiment A (Noise Sweep): Gaussian noise $\sigma \in \{0.00, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$ with outliers fixed at $0\%$.
  • Experiment B (Outlier Sweep): Uniform ambient outliers $\eta \in \{0\%, 5\%, 10\%, 20\%\}$ ($0, 15, 30, 60$ pts) with noise fixed at $0.00$.
  • Experiment C (Baseline): Clean matched baseline condition ($\sigma=0.00, \eta=0\%$).
  • Paired Trials: Seeds $\{11, 22, 33\}$ → identical coordinates fed to both algorithms → 144 evaluations.

• Evaluation Metrics & Inlier Protocol

  • Adjusted Rand Index (ARI): Chance-corrected partition agreement (Hubert & Arabie, 1985) in $[-1.0, 1.0]$. Expected value is $0.0$ for random labels; $1.0$ for perfect partition.
  • Inlier Evaluation Rule: ARI evaluated strictly across the $300$ ground-truth inliers. Prevents arbitrary outlier groupings from distorting scores.
  • Baseline Degradation: $\Delta\text{ARI} = \max(0.0, \text{ARI}_{\text{baseline}} - \text{ARI}_{\text{perturbed}})$. Measures non-negative performance loss.

Empirical Results

Experiment A: Gaussian Noise Sensitivity

Degradation dynamics across noise levels σ ∈ [0.00, 0.20]

• Key Numerical Findings

  • Gaussian Blobs: Both algorithms maintain perfect ARI = 1.0000 across all noise levels up to $\sigma = 0.20$ ($\Delta\text{ARI} = 0.0000$).
  • Two Moons: MST holds $\text{ARI} = 1.0000$ at $\sigma = 0.05$, drops to $0.6667$ at $\sigma = 0.10$, and collapses to 0.0000 at $\sigma = 0.20$ ($\Delta\text{ARI} = 1.0000$).
  • Concentric Circles: MST holds $\text{ARI} = 1.0000$ through $\sigma = 0.10$, but collapses to 0.0935 at $\sigma = 0.20$ ($\Delta\text{ARI} = 0.9065$).
  • Breakdown Threshold: MST collapses when noise dispersion creates inter-cluster shortcuts shorter than intra-cluster edges.

Empirical Discovery

Experiment B: Outlier Robustness & MST Cut Collapse

Pendant edge cut misallocation under uniform contamination

• The Central Finding: MST Collapse

  • Catastrophic Plunge: On Two Moons, adding just 5% uniform outliers (15 points) causes MST ARI to collapse from 1.0000 directly to 0.0000 ($\Delta\text{ARI} = 1.0000$).
  • K-Means Resilience: On Blobs, K-Means maintains ARI = 1.0000 up to 20% outliers, while MST degrades to $0.3799$.
  • The Mechanism (Pendant Edges): Outliers form long peripheral edges. The $(K-1)$ tree cuts isolate individual outliers into singleton clusters, leaving all $300$ inliers trapped in one giant component!

Synthesis & Practical Value

Conclusions & Practitioner Heuristic

Guiding algorithmic selection in noisy environments

• Decision Framework for Engineers

  • Spherical Clusters + Background Noise: → Choose K-Means (Robust centroid averaging; linear runtime).
  • Non-Convex Manifolds + Clean Data: → Choose MST Clustering (Perfect topological discovery).
  • Non-Convex Manifolds + Outliers: → Neither standard algorithm works! Use DBSCAN or outlier-trimmed MST.

• Limitations & Future Extensions

  • Experimental Limitations: Exploratory seed sample ($M=3$); 2D synthetic manifolds; browser timer quantization ($5\text{--}100\,\mu\text{s}$).
  • Future Extensions: Outlier-trimmed MST (density filtering prior to tree construction); sparse Delaunay triangulations ($O(N \log N)$); high-dimensional benchmarking ($D \gg 2$).
